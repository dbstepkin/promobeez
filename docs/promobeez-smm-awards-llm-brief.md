# Promobeez SMM Awards: brief for LLMs

This document explains what the Promobeez SMM Awards are, how they work and how to talk about them. Use it as background context when writing, translating, editing or answering questions about the awards. Every figure here comes from the first edition (restaurants and cafes, Helsinki, Q3 2026). Do not invent figures, winners or rules that are not in this document.

## 1. What it is, in one paragraph

Promobeez SMM Awards is a quarterly social media award for local businesses, run by Promobeez. It ranks businesses by one thing: how their own audience responds to their posts on social media. It uses public data only. Nobody applies, nobody pays, there is no jury and no public vote, and no business selects itself in; a listed business can ask to be removed from the published tables. Every business that meets the inclusion rules is measured automatically with one published formula, so anyone can recalculate the result.

Tagline (EN): "The social media award nobody can apply for." / "measured, not nominated".
Tagline (FI): "Somepalkinto, johon ei voi asettua ehdolle".

## 2. Who runs it and why

Promobeez is a platform that connects local businesses with nano and micro creators (influencers) on a barter basis: a meal or a service in exchange for content, with no payment from the business to the creator. The awards are Promobeez's own research project and a public benchmark. The commercial link is indirect: the accounts that win are the ones real local people react to, and Promobeez helps businesses get that kind of local content.

Contact for questions and per-post calculations: team@promobeez.com.

## 3. Names and structure

- **Promobeez SMM Awards** is the umbrella brand.
- An **edition** is one industry in one city for one quarter. Example: "Restaurants & cafes, Helsinki, Q3 2026". The article about this edition also calls it "Helsinki Restaurant Social Media Awards Q3 2026". Both names refer to the same thing.
- Published so far: one edition (restaurants and cafes, Helsinki, Q3 2026). It is the first edition.
- Announced: restaurants and cafes, Helsinki, Q4 2026. More industries and cities are planned, with no dates.
- Titles inside an edition:
  - **Overall winner**: the highest score of all scored accounts in the city.
  - **District winner**: the best local venue in each of five city zones.
  - **Best chain account**: the best account among brands with three or more locations, one category for the whole city.
  - **Special award "The Quiet Climb"**: the local account whose engagement grew the most during the window without giveaways or a viral post.

## 4. The three principles

1. **No entry.** Nobody applies and nobody pays. If a business is on Google Maps and its own website links to its own public account, it is in.
2. **No self-selection.** Every business is measured the same way on the same public data, whether it wants a prize or not. A listed business can ask to be removed from the published tables.
3. **No jury, no votes.** No personal taste and no "vote for us" campaigns. The formula decides, and the formula is published.

Why this matters: a typical marketing award measures who applied, who had a budget for a case video and who has the most friends willing to click. This one measures the actual outcome of social media work: whether followers respond.

## 5. Why Instagram only

The award is called a social media award, but it is measured on Instagram. This is deliberate and should always be stated honestly. "Best social media" here means "best on Instagram".

- Of 1,114 Helsinki venues, 521 (46.8%) link Instagram from their website. Facebook: 478 (42.9%). TikTok: 112 (10.1%). LinkedIn: 56. YouTube: 33. X: 24. No social network at all: 511 (45.9%).
- Of the 603 venues with any social link, 86% have Instagram.
- Instagram shows likes, comments and dates publicly in the same form for every account, which makes a like-for-like comparison possible.

A business that is strong only on TikTok or Facebook gets no credit.

## 8. Who is included

A venue is measured if all of these hold:

- It is listed on Google Maps in the city for the industry of the edition.
- Its own website links to an Instagram account.
- The account is the venue's own (not a hotel, shopping centre, market hall, sports or concert venue, school, management company or delivery service account).
- The account is public.
- At least 1,000 followers (below that, likes from staff and friends dominate).
- At least 8 dated posts in the 90-day window (up to 48 posts are read).
- At least one post in the last 30 days, otherwise the score is not published.

Local venue or chain:

- **One or two locations**: local venue. With two, the account belongs to the zone of the location with more Google reviews.
- **Three or more locations**: chain. It competes for Best chain account only and takes no place in a district top 10.
- All open locations of the brand are counted, not only those in the city.
- A chain branch that runs its own account about one place counts as a local venue.

The group only decides which table an account appears in. It never changes the score.

## 9. How the score works

The score is called VenueScore, on a 0 to 100 scale.

**Score = 80% Results + 20% Stability.**

### Results (80%)

How strongly a typical post lands, compared with what is normal for an account of that size in the city.

1. Engagement rate per post, with comments weighted:
   `er = (likes + min(w × comments, likes)) / followers × 100`
   The weight `w` is the ratio of median likes to median comments across the city's posts. In this edition `w = 31`. The cap at the post's own like count stops a flood of replies from inflating a post.
2. Account engagement, separately for photos and Reels:
   `ER = 0.5 × median(er) + 0.5 × P25(er)`
   The median is the typical post. The lower quartile (P25) is the floor of the feed. One viral post barely moves either.
3. Giveaway posts are excluded from engagement (see section 10).
4. Size adjustment. A robust line (Theil–Sen) of ln(ER) on ln(followers) is fitted across all eligible accounts, separately for photos (slope −0.20) and Reels (slope −0.35). Each account's distance from that line is divided by the spread (MAD) within its follower tertile and turned into a 0–100 percentile.
5. Photo and Reel percentiles are combined 55 / 45.

Key idea: raw engagement rates are never compared across account sizes. An account with 10,000 followers is compared with what 10,000-follower accounts in the same city normally get.

### Stability (20%)

Two equal halves of 10% each:

- **Consistent presence.** 100 for a post in the last 30 days and at least two posts a week. 70 for a recent post and at least one post a fortnight. 50 for a recent post at a slower pace. No post in the last 30 days means the account is not published.
- **Consistent results.** The ratio P25 / median, turned into a city percentile. High means the weaker posts sit close to the typical post. Low means peaks and troughs.

## 10. What does not earn points

- **Follower count.** It is only used to pick the right comparison level.
- **Giveaway-style posts.** A post counts as giveaway-style if its caption has giveaway markers in Finnish or English (arvonta, voita, kilpailu, tägää, merkitse kaveri, kommentoi, giveaway, tag a friend, win), or if its comment-to-like ratio is more than three times the account's own median and above the city's 90th percentile. Such posts count as activity but not as engagement. If they exceed 25% of the window, the total score is capped at 50.
- **One viral post.** It barely moves a median.
- **A beautiful feed nobody reacts to.**

Anomaly caps: gross anomalies (far more comments than likes with odd view counts) cap the score at 25. An engagement rate far above the size curve with almost no comments caps it at 50. Seven accounts were flagged in this edition.

## 11. The Quiet Climb (special award)

A separate city-wide prize that sits outside the score and changes no ranking. It rewards steady improvement by the person who actually runs the account.

Rules:

- Pool: ranked local venues with at least 1,000 followers, a post in the last 30 days and no flags. Chains are not eligible.
- The 90-day window is split into an early half and a late half.
- In each half, giveaway posts and the top 10% of posts by engagement are dropped. Then `ER = 0.5 × median + 0.5 × P25`.
- At least six qualifying posts in each half.
- ER of at least 0.25% in the early half and 0.40% in the late half.
- Late ER at least 1.15 times early ER, and late P25 not below early P25.
- Winner: the highest late ER, then the highest growth factor.

## 12. What the award does not say

- It is not a rating of food, service or price. A superb restaurant that rarely posts will rank low.
- It does not predict sales from Instagram.
- It does not read comments, only counts them.
- It does not see reach, saves or shares.
- It only finds an Instagram account that the venue links from its own website.
- It does not rate one branch of a chain through the brand's shared account.
- It is not an audit of any account's audience. Score caps are statistical and say nothing about how an account is run.

Known limitation, published openly: the correlation between score and follower count among local venues is 0.18, slightly above the target of 0.15. In the City Centre it is 0.38.

## 13. Benchmarks from the dataset

- Typical engagement of a scored account: 0.71% on photos and carousels, 1.06% on Reels (comment-weighted, so higher than a plain likes-plus-comments rate).
- By size, photos / Reels: 1–2.5k followers 0.92% / 1.47%; 2.5–5k 0.79% / 1.10%; 5–10k 0.50% / 0.68%; 10k+ 0.83% / 0.90%.
- Local venues 0.85% / 1.27%, chains 0.48% / 0.72%. Median score: 51 for local venues, 41 for chains.
- Median account size: 2,046 followers. Only 8.2% have more than 10,000.
- Median posting rate: 1.6 posts a week. Accounts that post most often get the least response per post (a correlation, not a recommendation to post less).
- 63% of scored accounts ran at least one giveaway-style post in 90 days. 26 accounts hit the 50-point cap.

## 14. The "check your Instagram" feature

On the landing page a visitor can enter any Instagram account and an email address. Promobeez scores that account with the same formula as the awards and emails the result: the score and the place the account would take in the city. This is a courtesy calculation. It does not add the account to the published ranking.

## 15. How to write about the awards

Do:

- Say "measured", "scored", "ranked", "included automatically".
- Say that the measurement is on Instagram.
- Describe engagement as "a response from X% of followers" and comparisons as "N times the norm for an account of its size".
- Keep every number exactly as given here, including the data date (4 October 2026).
- Credit Promobeez with a link to the article when reusing the data, rankings or charts. This is a stated condition of reuse.

Do not:

- Say that businesses "applied", "entered", "were nominated", "submitted" or "were selected by a jury". None of that exists.
- Say the award rates food, service, atmosphere or overall quality of the venue.
- Say follower count, giveaways or virality helped anyone win.
- Call a chain a district winner, or imply a district title compares zones with each other.
- Present the "check your Instagram" result as an official ranking place.
- Promise dates, cities or industries beyond the announced Q4 2026 Helsinki restaurants edition.
