Promobeez vs Vaiku: which influencer platform fits a local business in Finland?

Vaiku runs paid influencer campaigns from €149/month. Promobeez is a barter marketplace for local venues from €15. Pricing, creator pay, tracking and fit compared, checked October 2026.

Short answerBoth are self-serve and both are Finnish. Vaiku is a campaign platform built around paid collaborations: plans from €149 to €899 a month, contracts, payouts and content approval inside the platform, 1,280+ creators across Finland. Promobeez is a barter marketplace for local venues: you post an offer such as a dinner or a treatment, nearby nano and micro creators apply, and you pay a flat plan from €15 to €109 a month to unlock the contacts you choose. Pick Vaiku if you pay creators cash, sell online or need contracts and payouts handled. Pick Promobeez if you are a café, restaurant, salon or gym that wants local visits for the price of what you already sell.

Disclosure: Promobeez publishes this page, so read it as a vendor comparison. Every fact about Vaiku comes from vaiku.io, linked in the sources, as of 21 October 2026. Prices change; check both sites before you decide, and tell us if we got something wrong.

2. How Vaiku works

Vaiku calls itself a way to "run real influencer campaigns in Finland". A brand publishes a campaign with a budget and content needs, matched creators apply, and the brand handles messaging, contracts, content review and payouts in one dashboard. Vaiku says creators usually start applying within hours.[1]

Pricing is public on the brands page: Starter €149 a month for 1 campaign and 5 influencers, Growth €379 for 3 campaigns and 15 influencers, Pro €899 for 7 campaigns and 30 influencers, and a custom Enterprise plan with a dedicated account manager. Annual billing saves 20%. There are no setup fees, no per-campaign fees and no commission on campaign spend.[2]

For restaurants, Vaiku describes three tiers: nano creators under 10,000 followers "often work for a complimentary meal or product seeding", micro creators (10,000 to 100,000) typically charge €200 to €500 per campaign, and macro creators €500 to €2,000 or more. It tracks restaurant campaigns with unique booking codes.[3]

Vaiku has an iOS app and a case study with Helsingin Makkaratehdas, which picked Vaiku over a €27,000 agency proposal.[1]

3. How Promobeez works

Promobeez is a barter marketplace. A business posts an offer, for example "lunch for two for a Reel and Stories". Creators with a local audience apply. Before you pay anything, you see each applicant's engagement, how much of their audience is local, their content profile and how recently they did commercial posts. Identity stays hidden until you unlock.

You unlock the contacts you like and agree the visit directly. After the visit, Promobeez watches the published post so you can see that the barter was honoured. Creators never pay.[4]

The bigger difference is on top of the platform fee. On Vaiku's own figures, a micro creator costs €200 to €500 per campaign.[3] On Promobeez the deal is the offer: if a dinner for two costs you €25 in food, that is the campaign.

That does not make one cheaper in every case. A cash fee buys guarantees a barter cannot: fixed deliverables, usage rights, deadlines. Vaiku also handles the contract and payout for you.

5. Paying creators: cash vs barter

Cash fits when you need reach outside your own neighbourhood, a fixed number of posts, rights to reuse the video in ads, or creators above 10,000 followers, who mostly expect a fee.

Barter fits when the product is the experience: a meal, a treatment, a class. The creator has to visit, so the content shows your place, and their audience is near enough to come too. Small local creators often reach more people who could actually visit than a larger creator with a national audience. See why audiences trust small creators more than brands.

Both are advertising under Finnish rules and both need a clear disclosure in the post. See influencer marketing disclosure in Finland.

6. When Vaiku is the better choice

  • You pay creators and want contracts, approvals and payouts in one place.
  • You sell online and care about clicks, conversions and sales per creator.
  • You need reach across Finland, not one district.
  • You want creators above 10,000 followers or a guaranteed set of deliverables.
  • You run several campaigns a month and want an account manager (Enterprise).

7. When Promobeez is the better choice

  • You are a local venue (café, restaurant, salon, gym, studio) and want people from your area to visit.
  • You want to start small with €15, refunded if fewer than 3 creators apply, and see how many creators want your offer before you subscribe.
  • You would rather give a meal than send money.
  • You want to choose by local audience, not follower count.
  • You want to keep the creator contact and invite the good ones back next season.

8. FAQ

How much does Vaiku cost?

Starter €149, Growth €379 and Pro €899 a month plus VAT, with 20% off on annual billing, and a custom Enterprise plan. No setup fee and no commission.[2]

How much does Promobeez cost?

€15 once for one offer with 3 unlocks (refunded in one click if fewer than 3 creators apply in 14 days), then Starter €69 or Growth €109 a month. New businesses get 14 days of Growth free.[4]

Is there a Vaiku alternative for small restaurants?

Promobeez is built for that case: a barter offer instead of a campaign budget, a €15 entry plan with money back, and creators ranked by local audience. If you pay creators cash, Vaiku remains a strong Finnish option.

Does Vaiku support barter?

Vaiku's restaurant page says nano creators often work for a free meal or product, so a gifted visit is possible there too. Its plans are priced per campaign and per influencer, and the platform is built around paid collaborations.[3]

Can I use Vaiku and Promobeez together?

Yes. Some businesses run paid campaigns with larger creators for a launch and keep a barter offer open on Promobeez for steady local posts between launches.
