Best restaurant social media in Helsinki, Q3 2026: the award nobody can apply for

We went looking for the best restaurant social media in Helsinki and measured it where most restaurants actually are: on Instagram. Every restaurant and cafe we could find on Google Maps, ranked by one thing: how the audience responds. No entry form, no jury, no public vote. Here are the winners in five districts, the numbers behind them, and the method anyone can check.

The short answer. The best restaurant social media in Helsinki in Q3 2026 belongs to Friends & Brgrs (92.2 / 100), the overall winner and the best chain account. Among local restaurants Savoy leads with 91.2, ahead of Villa Severino (90.7) and Restaurant Elite (89.1). The five district winners are Savoy (City Centre), Villa Severino (East), Restaurant Elite (West & Northwest), Helkatti Cat Cafe (South Central) and Oljenkorsi (Kallio & North Central). The award is measured on Instagram, the network most Helsinki restaurants use. It is built only from public data on 246 qualifying accounts and scores two things: results, meaning audience response relative to account size (80%), and the stability of the feed (20%).

Why these accounts won

The score answers one narrow question: does this venue's Instagram get a real response from the people who follow it, and is the account actually being run? It has two blocks with fixed weights: results, 80%, and stability, 20%. Stability is about consistency: half of it is whether the account keeps showing up, half is whether its results hold from post to post.

  • 80% ResultsHow strongly a typical post lands, compared with what is normal for an account of that size in Helsinki.
  • 10% Stability: consistent presenceWhether the result is still being produced: the account is active now and keeps a steady rhythm.
  • 10% Stability: consistent resultsWhether the weaker posts hold up too, or the average is carried by a few spikes.

The block that matters most is Results, and the key idea in it is that raw engagement rates are never compared across account sizes. A 2,000-follower account at 1.5% and a 20,000-follower account at 1.5% are not doing equally well, because engagement falls as accounts grow. So each account is compared with the curve of what is typical for its size across Helsinki.

Savoy is the clearest case. An account with 11,000 followers in this city typically gets a response from about 0.5% of followers on a photo post. Savoy gets 3.2%, more than six times the expected level, and it holds up on its weaker posts too. Oljenkorsi wins Kallio & North Central with 1,523 followers for the same reason at the other end of the scale: its Reels reach 5.4% of followers where 1.3% would be normal.

What does not earn points Follower count. Giveaways and "tag a friend" posts, which are removed from the calculation. One viral post, which barely moves a median. A beautiful feed nobody reacts to.

An award you cannot enter, and cannot skip

Most marketing awards work the same way. A venue or its agency fills in an entry form, often pays a fee, and a jury or a public vote decides. That design measures who applied, who has a budget for a case video, and who has the most friends willing to click. It says little about the quiet neighbourhood place whose social media simply works.

As far as we know, this is the first restaurant social media award in Helsinki that removes all of that.

No entryNobody applies and nobody pays. If a venue is on Google Maps and its website links a public Instagram account of its own, it is in.
No opt-outA venue cannot stay out to avoid a bad result. Everyone is measured on the same public data.
No jury, no votesNo personal taste, no friends of the organiser, no "vote for us" campaigns. The formula decides, and the formula is published below.

What it rewards is the actual outcome of social media work: whether followers respond. That is why the result is fully reproducible. Take any account in the tables, open its last three months of posts, apply the formulas in the methodology, and you should land on the same number.

Why the award is measured on Instagram

The title says social media and the measurement is Instagram. That is a deliberate narrowing, and it should be said out loud: Instagram is the one network that the largest number of Helsinki restaurants actually use.

521 of the 1,114 venues link an Instagram account from their own website, ahead of Facebook at 478. TikTok follows far behind with 112 venues (10%), then LinkedIn with 56, YouTube with 33, X with 24, WhatsApp with 10 and Pinterest with 4. 511 venues (46%) link no social network at all.

The networks also overlap heavily. 407 venues link both Instagram and Facebook, and 105 of the 112 venues on TikTok have an Instagram as well. Of the 603 venues with any social link, 86% have Instagram. A ranking built on any other network would leave most of the field out.

There is a practical reason too. Instagram shows likes, comments and dates for every post in public, in the same form for every account, which is what makes a like-for-like comparison possible. So wherever this article says "best social media", read it as best on Instagram. A restaurant that is strong only on TikTok or Facebook gets no credit for it here.

Helsinki restaurant Instagram in numbers

The same dataset doubles as a benchmark for anyone running a restaurant account in Helsinki. These are the figures we would want on the wall.

Fewer than half of Helsinki restaurants link an Instagram

Of 1,114 venues, 944 (85%) have a website and 521 (46.8%) link an Instagram account from it. The share differs sharply from one part of the city to another.

The gap between the centre and the east is close to threefold. In the City Centre almost two venues in three have an Instagram; in East Helsinki it is fewer than one in four. For a restaurant in the east this is an opening: the local field is nearly empty.

Venues with an Instagram also have more of a footprint on Google: a median of 419 reviews against 219 for venues without one, while their average rating is almost identical (4.36 against 4.31).

Half of the accounts fail a low bar

Having an account and running an account are different things. 29% of restaurant Instagram accounts in Helsinki have fewer than 1,000 followers, and another 16% posted fewer than eight times in three months.

Most accounts are small

The engagement benchmark: 0.7% on photos, 1.1% on Reels

Reels beat photos in every size band, and inside individual accounts too: in 109 of the 175 accounts that post both formats (62%), the typical Reel out-engages the typical photo. The dip at 5,000–10,000 followers and the recovery above 10,000 is why the ranking uses a size curve and separate spread estimates per size band, not a simple "smaller is better" rule.

How often restaurants post

The median ranked account posts 1.6 times a week. 35% post at least twice a week and have posted in the last 30 days; 63% post between once a fortnight and twice a week; 3% have gone quiet for more than a month.

The accounts that post most often get the least response per post. That does not mean posting less is a strategy, but it does mean volume on its own is not one either.

Local venues out-engage chains

The typical local venue gets a response from 0.85% of followers on a photo post and 1.27% on a Reel. For chains the figures are 0.48% and 0.72%, even though chains post slightly more often (1.8 posts a week against 1.5).

Giveaways are everywhere

155 of 246 scored accounts (63%) published at least one post in 90 days that reads as a giveaway or comment bait, and for the median account such posts make up 8% of the feed. For 26 accounts (11%) they make up more than a quarter, which caps their score at 50.

Methodology

This section is the full method. It is long on purpose: the claim that the award is transparent only holds if a marketer or analyst can reproduce it.

  1. 1Find the venues1,114 restaurants and cafes on Google Maps, searched district by district.
  2. 2Find the InstagramEach venue's own website is crawled for its social links. 521 venues link an Instagram.
  3. 3Sort the accountsLocal venue, chain with three or more locations, or not the venue's own account. The rules do not look at the score.
  4. 4Read 90 days of postsLikes, comments, Reels views and dates for every public account: 4,092 posts.
  5. 5FilterAt least 1,000 followers, 8 dated posts in the window, public account.
  6. 6Score and rankOne city-wide calibration, then a top 10 inside each zone.

1. The sample

We collected restaurants and cafes from Google Maps for every district of Helsinki: 1,114 venues across the five zones. For each venue we went through its own website and picked up the social links.

2. Whose account is it: local venues, chains and exclusions

A district ranking answers the question "whose Instagram works best in this part of town". For the answer to be fair, the account has to represent a venue in that part of town. So every account with 1,000 or more followers is put in one of three groups, by rules that do not look at its score. The group does not change the score either. It only decides which table the account is shown in.

GroupWho is in itWhere it is shown
Local venueThe venue's own account; the brand has one or two locationsDistrict top 10 and city top 10
ChainThe brand's own account; the brand has three or more locationsBest chain account, one category for the whole city
Not rankedThe account does not belong to the venue, or the main location is not in HelsinkiNowhere

Step 1: is it the venue's own account? It is, if it is run in the name of the venue or its brand and is about its food, its drinks and its guests. It is not, if it belongs to the place where the venue is or to whoever operates it: a hotel, a shopping centre or market hall, a sports or concert venue, a school, a management company, a delivery service. If a venue's website links only to such an account, we treat the venue as having no Instagram of its own and it is not ranked. The followers and the response on that account belong to the hotel or the hall as a whole and say nothing about the restaurant.

Step 2: how many locations does the brand have? We count every open location of the brand, not only those in Helsinki, using the brand's own list of restaurants, Google Maps and the location tags on its posts.

  • One or two locations: a local venue. With two, the account belongs to the zone of the location with more Google reviews. The review count is a simple public signal of which one is more prominent, so nobody hand-picks a flagship.
  • Three or more: a chain. The account competes for Best chain account and does not take a place in a district top 10.

The line is drawn at three because that is where an account stops being the voice of one place: the content is made for the brand and the audience is gathered from several districts or cities. Chain scores use the same formula and the same city-wide scale, so they can be read against the district scores. There is one chain category for the whole city, since a chain has no district of its own.

One question settles almost every borderline account: what does this account post about on an ordinary Tuesday? If it is dishes, tables and guests of this place, it is the venue's own. If it is hotel rooms, gym memberships, concerts or ten cities at once, it is not.

Every group is checked against the brand's own website, with a recorded reason, a location count and a check date. It depends on two things only: who runs the account and how many locations the brand has. Of the 246 scored accounts, 172 are local venues, 53 are chain accounts and 21 were set aside: 9 hotel accounts, 7 accounts of other businesses and organisations such as a sports centre, a concert hall and a college, 2 market halls, 1 delivery platform and 2 brands whose main restaurant is in another city. Seven of the local accounts had not posted in the last 30 days and are left out of the published tables, which leaves 165 local accounts in the district ranking.

4. Results (80%)

For every post, photos, carousels and Reels alike, we compute a comment-weighted engagement rate against followers:

er = (likes + min(w × comments, likes)) / followers × 100

The comment weight w is not chosen by hand. It is the ratio of median likes to median comments across the city's posts, which came out at w = 31, so that in aggregate comments count as much as likes. The cap at the post's own like count stops a flood of replies from inflating a post.

An account's engagement is then the average of its median post and its lower-quartile post, separately for photos and Reels:

ER = 0.5 × median(er) + 0.5 × P25(er)

The median is the typical post; the lower quartile is the floor of the feed. To score well, the weaker quarter of posts has to hold up as well. One contest post or one viral Reel barely moves either number.

Giveaway posts are excluded. A post is treated as bait if its caption contains giveaway markers in Finnish or English (arvonta, voita, kilpailu, tägää, merkitse kaveri, kommentoi, giveaway, tag a friend, win), or if its comment-to-like ratio is more than three times the account's own median and above the city's 90th percentile. Such posts still count as activity, but not for engagement. If they exceed 25% of the window, the total score is capped at 50.

Size adjustment. Across all eligible accounts we fit a robust line (Theil–Sen) of ln(ER) on ln(followers), separately for photos and Reels. For photos the slope is −0.20 and for Reels −0.35: a tenfold larger account has about 36% lower photo engagement and 55% lower Reel engagement. Each account's distance from that line is divided by the spread (MAD) within its follower tertile and turned into a 0–100 percentile. Photo and Reel percentiles are combined 55 / 45.

So an account with 10,000 followers and 1.3% is not compared with a 1,500-follower account at 4%. It is compared with what 10,000-follower restaurant accounts in Helsinki normally get.

5. Stability, part one: consistent presence (10%)

Stability is the second block of the score, worth 20% in total. It measures consistency and has two equal halves. The first asks whether the result is still being produced, and guards against the showcase account that built an audience years ago and stopped posting. It has four levels: 100 for a post in the last 30 days and at least two posts a week; 70 for a recent post and at least one post a fortnight; 50 for a recent post at a slower pace. An account with no post in the last 30 days is not published at all.

6. Stability, part two: consistent results (10%)

The second half guards against spikes. Evenness is the ratio of the lower-quartile post to the median post, P25 / median, turned into a city percentile. A high value means the floor of the feed sits close to the typical post. A low value means peaks and troughs.

7. Anomaly caps

Authenticity is not a weight, because it cannot be measured precisely from public data. Instead, flags limit the maximum score: gross anomalies (far more comments than likes together with odd view counts) cap the score at 25; an engagement rate more than two deviations above the size curve with almost no comments caps it at 50; a giveaway-heavy feed caps it at 50. Seven accounts were flagged as suspect in this edition.

8. Fairness checks

A size-adjusted score should not correlate with size. We test that on the local venues that make up the district rankings and publish the result, including where it falls short.

  • City-wide: Spearman ρ between score and followers is 0.18 across 165 local accounts, above the target of |ρ| < 0.15. Larger local accounts score slightly higher on average.
  • By zone: South Central 0.14, Kallio & North Central 0.09, West & Northwest 0.06 and East 0.14 (seven accounts) are inside the target. The City Centre, at 0.38, is well above it: among its 35 local accounts the larger ones clearly score higher.
  • Top by size band: the city top 10 holds two accounts from the smallest third of accounts, three from the middle and five from the largest, so the largest third is slightly over our 20–45% guide range. In the top 20 the split is 4 / 7 / 9, inside the range.
  • Comment weight sensitivity: recalculating with w halved keeps 8 of the city top 10; with w at 1.5× it keeps 9. First place does not change in any variant.

What this ranking does not say

  • It is not a rating of food, service or price. A superb restaurant that rarely posts will rank low, because this measures Instagram and nothing else.
  • It does not predict sales from Instagram.
  • It does not read comments. Only the count is available, so a real conversation and a row of emojis look the same, which is why bait posts are removed by caption and ratio.
  • It does not see reach, saves or shares. Those live in each venue's own Meta Insights.
  • It only finds an Instagram that the venue links from its own website. A venue with an unlinked account is missing.
  • It is not a rating of hotels, halls and operators. A venue without an account of its own is not ranked, however good the account of the building it sits in.
  • It does not rate a single branch of a chain through the brand's shared account. Chains are compared with each other.
  • It catches only crude manipulation. It is not a full audit of fake followers.

How to check our work

  1. Sample. Is every venue in a zone with a linked Instagram in the starting list?
  2. Groups. For any account in a top 10, open the brand's website and count its locations (one or two, or three and more), and open the profile to see that it is run in the venue's name. Every group has a recorded reason, and you can ask us for it.
  3. Window. One cut-off date for everyone: 4 October 2026.
  4. Recalculate. Take two accounts from the top and two from the bottom of a table and rebuild their medians in a spreadsheet.
  5. Size. Check that score and follower count do not move together.
  6. Robustness. Remove the winner's single best post. It should still be in the top 10.
  7. Falsifiability. If the venues with the best guest experience in a district sit at the bottom only because they post rarely, then this measures Instagram hygiene and not the quality of the place. We say so openly: that is exactly its scope.

FAQ

Which restaurant has the best social media in Helsinki in Q3 2026?

Friends & Brgrs (@friendsandbrgrs) is the overall winner of the Helsinki Restaurant Social Media Awards Q3 2026, which are measured on Instagram: 92.2 out of 100, the highest score of all 246 accounts. It also wins Best chain account. Among local restaurants Savoy (@savoyhelsinki) leads with 91.2, followed by Villa Severino (90.7), Restaurant Elite (89.1), Ravintola Palace (86.2) and Helkatti Cat Cafe (85.5). Accounts are scored on results (audience response relative to account size, 80%) and the stability of the feed (20%) over the 90 days to 4 October 2026.

Why are chains ranked separately?

From the third location on, an Instagram account stops being the voice of one place: the content is made for a brand and the audience comes from several districts or cities. Comparing it with a cafe whose followers live around the corner is unfair in both directions. Brands with three or more locations therefore compete for Best chain account, one city-wide category on the same scale. Brands with one or two locations are ranked as local venues.

Why are the awards based on Instagram only?

Instagram is the social network most Helsinki restaurants use: 521 of 1,114 venues (46.8%) link it from their website, against 478 for Facebook, 112 for TikTok, 56 for LinkedIn and 33 for YouTube. Of the venues that link any social network, 86% link Instagram. It also shows comparable public numbers for every post, so all accounts can be measured the same way.

How can a restaurant enter the awards?

It cannot, and that is the point. Every Helsinki restaurant and cafe found on Google Maps whose website links to a public Instagram account of its own is included automatically. There is no entry form, no fee, no jury and no public vote. A venue also cannot opt out of being measured, because only public data is used.

What is a good Instagram engagement rate for a restaurant in Helsinki?

Among 246 scored Helsinki restaurant accounts the typical post reaches 0.71% of followers for photos and carousels and 1.06% for Reels, using a comment-weighted engagement rate. Accounts with 1,000 to 2,500 followers typically see 0.92% on photos and 1.47% on Reels; accounts with 5,000 to 10,000 followers see 0.50% and 0.68%.

Why does follower count not decide the ranking?

Followers can be bought or accumulated years ago, and larger accounts always show lower engagement rates. The score therefore compares each account's engagement with what is normal for its size in Helsinki. Among local venues the correlation between score and follower count is 0.18, and the five district winners range from 1,523 to 13,084 followers.

How many Helsinki restaurants are on Instagram?

Of 1,114 restaurants and cafes in the study, 521 (46.8%) link an Instagram account from their own website. The share is 64% in the City Centre, 60% in South Central and only 23% in East Helsinki. 478 venues (42.9%) link a Facebook page and 112 (10%) link TikTok.

Do giveaways help a restaurant win?

No. Posts that look like giveaways or comment bait are removed from the engagement calculation, and an account where more than a quarter of posts are of that kind has its score capped at 50. 63% of scored accounts ran at least one such post in 90 days; 26 hit the cap.

How often is the ranking updated?

This is the first edition and it covers the third quarter of 2026: the 90 days up to the data cut-off on 4 October 2026. The plan is to repeat it every quarter. The method is fixed in advance and published in full, so the next quarter can be compared with this one.
